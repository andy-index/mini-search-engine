Title: Computer Networks: HTTP/3 and QUIC Protocol Overview

HTTP/3 represents the latest evolution of the Hypertext Transfer Protocol, shifting transport layer foundations from TCP to QUIC (Quick UDP Internet Connections). Traditional HTTP/2 over TCP suffers from head-of-line blocking at the transport layer, where a single lost packet stalls all multiplexed streams. QUIC operates over UDP and introduces independent stream multiplexing, eliminating cross-stream blocking entirely. Furthermore, QUIC integrates Transport Layer Security (TLS 1.3) directly into the handshake, significantly reducing connection establishment latency. It also supports robust connection migration, allowing mobile clients to switch seamlessly between Wi-Fi and cellular networks without dropping active sessions. These advancements make HTTP/3 faster, more resilient, and better optimized for modern web traffic.
