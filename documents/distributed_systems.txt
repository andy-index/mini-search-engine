Distributed Computing Concepts

Distributed systems consist of autonomous computing nodes connected over networks that communicate and coordinate actions by passing messages to achieve a unified goal. Distributed architectures provide high availability, fault tolerance, and horizontal scalability. Fundamental challenges in distributed systems involve consensus mechanisms, network latency, network partitions, and clock synchronization. The CAP theorem states that a distributed data store can simultaneously provide at most two of three guarantees: Consistency, Availability, and Partition Tolerance. Consensus algorithms such as Paxos and Raft ensure state machine replication across nodes even during network failures. Modern distributed systems power web-scale storage, distributed databases, streaming platforms, and microservices.
