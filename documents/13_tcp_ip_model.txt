Title: Computer Networks: The TCP/IP Model and Packet Encapsulation

The Transmission Control Protocol / Internet Protocol (TCP/IP) suite is the foundational communication architecture of the modern internet. Structured into four functional layers—Link, Internet, Transport, and Application—it standardizes how data is formatted, addressed, transmitted, and received across disparate networks. Packet encapsulation occurs as data descends the stack: application payloads are wrapped in transport headers (TCP/UDP), encapsulated in IP packets with source and destination addresses, and finally framed into physical link-layer frames. TCP provides reliable, connection-oriented delivery with flow control and error checking, whereas UDP offers connectionless, low-latency transmission suited for real-time streaming and gaming. Understanding this layered encapsulation model is essential for network engineering and troubleshooting.
