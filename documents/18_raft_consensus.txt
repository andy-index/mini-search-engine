Title: Distributed Systems: The Raft Consensus Algorithm Explained

Achieving consensus among distributed nodes is essential for fault-tolerant state machine replication. The Raft consensus algorithm was designed as an understandable alternative to Paxos, decomposing consensus into distinct sub-problems: leader election, log replication, and safety. In a Raft cluster, nodes exist in one of three states: follower, candidate, or leader. Clients send requests exclusively to the leader, which appends entries to its local log and replicates them to followers via AppendEntries RPCs. Once a log entry is safely replicated on a majority of nodes, it is committed and applied to the state machine. Raft's rigorous safety invariants ensure that elected leaders possess all committed log entries, guaranteeing strong consistency across distributed nodes.
