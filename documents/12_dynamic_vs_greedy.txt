Title: Algorithms: Dynamic Programming vs. Greedy Algorithms

Designing efficient algorithms often requires choosing between dynamic programming and greedy paradigms based on the problem structure. Dynamic programming solves complex problems by breaking them down into overlapping subproblems, storing results in a table to avoid redundant computations (memoization or tabulation). This approach guarantees globally optimal solutions for problems exhibiting optimal substructure. Conversely, greedy algorithms make locally optimal choices at each step with the hope of finding a global optimum. While greedy algorithms are faster and simpler—such as Dijkstra's algorithm for shortest paths or Huffman coding—they do not work for all problems. Identifying whether a problem possesses the greedy choice property is key to selecting the correct algorithmic strategy.
