Title: Algorithms: Sorting Algorithms Complexity Analysis

Sorting algorithms are foundational tools in computer science, organizing data elements into specific numerical or lexicographical order. Comparison-based sorting algorithms, such as Merge Sort and Quick Sort, operate with a theoretical lower bound of $O(n \log n)$ time complexity. Merge Sort uses a divide-and-conquer approach, guaranteeing stable $O(n \log n)$ performance across all cases at the cost of additional memory overhead. Quick Sort partitions arrays around a pivot element, offering exceptional average-case performance and in-place sorting, though its worst-case time complexity can degrade to $O(n^2)$ with poor pivot choices. Non-comparison sorts like Radix Sort can achieve $O(n)$ time under specific constraints, illustrating the trade-offs inherent in algorithmic design.
