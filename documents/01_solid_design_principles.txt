Title: Software Engineering Principles: SOLID Design in Practice

SOLID principles form the bedrock of robust object-oriented software engineering. Single Responsibility Principle ensures that a class has only one reason to change, promoting modularity and maintainability. Open/Closed Principle dictates that software entities should be open for extension but closed for modification, preventing unintended regressions. Liskov Substitution Principle guarantees that derived classes must be substitutable for their base classes without altering correct program behavior. Interface Segregation Principle advocates for client-specific interfaces rather than a monolithic general-purpose interface, reducing coupling. Finally, Dependency Inversion Principle mandates depending upon abstractions rather than concrete implementations, facilitating decoupled, easily testable architectures. Mastering these five tenets allows software engineers to build extensible systems that gracefully adapt to evolving business requirements over time.
