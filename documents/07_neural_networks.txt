Title: Machine Learning: Introduction to Neural Networks and Activation Functions

Neural networks form the computational backbone of modern machine learning and deep learning architectures. Inspired by biological nervous systems, artificial neural networks consist of interconnected layers of artificial neurons, comprising an input layer, hidden layers, and an output layer. Each connection has an associated weight that adjusts during the training process via backpropagation and gradient descent. Activation functions introduce crucial non-linearity into the network, enabling models to learn complex, non-trivial patterns in data. Common activation functions include ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) for hidden layers, Sigmoid for binary classification outputs, and Softmax for multi-class probability distributions. Without non-linear activation functions, a deep neural network would collapse mathematically into a simple linear regression model.
